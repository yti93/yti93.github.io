Play It As It Lays

Joan Didion · ★★★★☆ · Aug 2026

Ennui is a series of rambling drives from San Pedro to Palm Desert, from Long Beach to Ventura and Oxnard, and back again, without discovering much about anything as the tires chew up the miles.

First Joan Didion novel, felt like this book was a breath of fresh air, with how sparsely and matter of factly it was written, every event is left up to the reader themselves to assign relative importance. The 60s were truly a wild time and Play It As It Lays to me, outlines how difficult it must have been to keep sane in the tumultuous, sun-kissed yet drug-tinged hills of Hollywood, especially as a woman. I would say that's showbiz baby, but i know nothing about showbiz.

This might piss some people off but this is the book that Otessa Moshfegh thought she was writing when she wrote My Year of Rest and Relaxation.

The Minotaur Takes a Cigarette Break

Steven Sherrill · ★★★★☆ · Aug 2026

I thought I had read this book many years ago but suppose not.

An odd little book that I enjoyed, mostly by thinking about it in the couple hours after each reading session. I desperately tried to think about the broader point the book was trying to make - is the Minotaur an approximation of man? Of the faults and imperfections that separate mortals from immortals, represented by an individual that straddles both sides? Is it something deeper than that that I'm missing?

Ultimately the humdrum nature of the Minotaur's life, the monotony of this (cigarette break) of his life compared to the 5,000 years of life before it (which also seemed monotonous), seem to be the point - at least the point I'm taking away from it. Life is the point, living is the point. There is no grand finale, no blockbuster payoff - sometimes you just live a life and that's the beauty of it - whether you're carving prime rib or fixing a car or going through your nightly self-care routine. There's always something to look forward to, like that cigarette.

A Parade of Horribles (Dungeon Crawler Carl, #8)

Matt Dinniman · ★★★★☆ · Jun 2026

[ADVANCE COPY REVIEW]

"You will not break me."

There's a gradual shift in the tenor of DCC from LitRPG hijinks (skills! loot boxes! mobs!) to a more contemplative rumination of what it means to be human and maintain your sense of self and your sense of community in times of extreme duress and oppression. We start to see that shift after the Butcher's Masquerade yet I think A Parade of Horribles is when we concretely understand this isn't just a game anymore. The 10th level mechanism is simplistic which allows for the story development to flourish. This does not mean, however, that the mechanics of the game take a backseat - on the contrary, the butterfly effect of the Crawlers trying to break the dungeon and bend the rules of the game is what moves this story forward. I wouldn't be surprised if we move forward outside of the dungeon to wrap up this series. 5/5, will re-read advance copy, listen to the audiobook, and read the official copy in May.

Dark Age (Red Rising Saga, #5)

Pierce Brown · ★★★★★ · Jan 2026

Even going into this book, the title made me consider that this would be the heaviest book of the series so far and boy did it deliver. The bloodiest book in the series with long protracted war scenes for the first time really opened up the world beyond Darrow’s battles. Positives of the book include a flushing out of the alternative points of view and narrators in introduced in the last book as well as tension between doing what is right and doing what is good for yourself, your allies, and humanity. These are not your institutes new initiates anymore. They are adults hardened war, veterans, and people with difficult choices to make. You can’t please everyone as it seems each main character has had to make a decision they can’t live with

The Serviceberry: Abundance and Reciprocity in the Natural World

Robin Wall Kimmerer · ★★★★☆ · Jan 2026

This book was a very timely read as we enter 2026. With all of the capitalist- motivated actions of an increasing amount of governments, including our own, the author introduces and explores the idea of alternative economies specifically the gift economy. Group over self, reciprocity over selfishness, and dismantling preconceived notions of what it means to be both successful and happy as an individual and as part of a functioning society are all great reminders to keep others in your heart. Finished listening to this book as I head to my fun little capitalist job. Would recommend and at a two hour audiobook. It’s a great listen.

This Inevitable Ruin (Dungeon Crawler Carl, #7)

Matt Dinniman · ★★★★★ · Jan 2026

After reading this whole series in <1 month, and thinking about what rocks about this book and this series, here are 5 thoughts: - it's just a really fun concept and a blast to read overall, and I think that's underrated. Read what makes you happy, and this makes me happy - the political and socioeconomic commentary is not heavy handed but there's just enough to make you pause and think about where you've seen this in real life before - i have played a fair amount of RPGs and watched a fair amount of reality tv and I love the world-building. It reminds me of Survivor, Fallout, GTA, and Squid Game - Easter Eggs abound - there was a Die Hard reference that I caught only because Die Hard was playing in the background, and I'm sure I've missed about 90% of all Easter Eggs - The concepts generally work, but I will say that sometimes the sheer amount of details are overwhelming. This is a double-edged sword - Dinniman does a pretty good job of having a character or narrative explanation of what transpired and why it was important, or what the callback was, but still rough. However, it does leave room for my dumb ass t0 continue being surprised by a plot device placed 2.5 novels ago in a throwaway AI comment.

I think what also calls to me especially with the 7th installment is the shift in tone, the somber tone finally dominating rather than the wacky comic relief. [spoilers removed]

Overall would recommend to any and all compensated anarchists and former child actors out there
